    it is 0 by default (snapshot used as-is).


2. WHY POLES ARE GROUPED INTO LINE SECTIONS
-------------------------------------------
Utilities usually install poles as part of a project (a new subdivision, a
line rebuild or extension, a road widening), so a whole stretch of line gets
poles of the same age. After that, single poles are replaced one at a time
over the decades (failed inspections, car hits, storm damage).

If every pole's age were drawn independently, every part of the feeder would
get the same mix of old and new poles. That gives the right failure
probability for each pole, but it hides the fact that an old section fails
together and takes out everything downstream. For system-level results
(outages, crews) that matters, so ages are assigned by section.

How a line section is defined
  The spans table says which two poles each span connects. A pole with 3 or
  more spans is a junction. A line section is a chain of poles between
  junctions (or line ends). Each junction pole joins the largest section it
  touches. This uses the network connections, not x/y distance, because two
  poles can be close together but on different lines built decades apart.

  Feeder 1878 (suburban): 1,432 poles in 344 line sections
  (median 2 poles per section, largest 36). Sections are short because the
  polegen2 network has many short stubs (506 end poles).


3. HOW EACH POLE GETS ITS AGE
-----------------------------
Step A - Spot replacements
  Each pole has a 20% chance (rho = 0.20) of being a "spot replacement". Its
  age is drawn on its own from the Alectra histogram (pick a bin using the
  percentages above, then a whole-number age inside that bin), independent of
  its section.

Step B - Section cohorts
  Every other pole takes its section's installation age:
  * Sections are processed in random order. Each one picks an age bin with
    probability proportional to how many poles that bin still needs to match
    the Alectra percentages. This keeps the feeder's overall histogram close
    to Alectra's even though there are only a few hundred sections.
  * The section gets one age inside that bin.
  * Each pole in the section gets that age +/- up to 2 years (jitter), since
    a project is not all installed on the same day.

Both steps draw from the same histogram, so the overall age distribution
matches Alectra whatever value rho takes. rho only controls how strongly ages
are grouped by section:
  rho = 0   -> pure cohorts (every pole matches its section)
  rho = 1   -> fully independent (no grouping)

Reproducibility: AGE_SEED = 2026 gives the same ages every run.


4. HOW rho = 0.20 WAS DERIVED
-----------------------------
Source: the same Alectra DSP, Appendix B01 (Overhead Asset Renewal). Alectra
replaced 3,259 poles from 2021 to 2024 under its Pole Renewal program. These
are individual poles flagged by inspection and residual-strength testing
(CSA C22.3: replace or reinforce at 60% of design strength), which is the
kind of one-at-a-time replacement rho stands for.

Step 1 - yearly replacement rate
  3,259 poles / 4 years = about 815 poles per year
  815 / 105,262 wood poles = 0.77% of poles per year

Step 2 - chance a pole has been swapped after t years
  Each year a pole has a 99.23% chance of NOT being replaced, so after t years
  the share swapped is 1 - 0.9923^t.
  Example: a 30-year-old section -> 1 - 0.9923^30 = about 21% swapped.

Step 3 - average over the Alectra age histogram
  For each bin, use the middle age, compute the share swapped, and multiply
  by the number of poles in the bin.

  Age bin   Middle age   Poles    % swapped   Expected swapped poles
  0-5          2.5       10,603      1.9%           203
  6-10         8         11,447      6.0%           686
  11-15       13          9,116      9.6%           872
  16-20       18          8,871     13.0%         1,152
  21-25       23          8,452     16.3%         1,377
  26-30       28          8,463     19.5%         1,647
  31-35       33          7,708     22.5%         1,735
  36-40       38          5,882     25.5%         1,497
  41-45       43          4,415     28.3%         1,249
  46-50       48          4,228     31.0%         1,311
  51-55       53          4,756     33.6%         1,599
  56-60       58          3,549     36.1%         1,282
  61-65       63          3,220     38.6%         1,241
  66-70       68          3,726     40.9%         1,523
  71-75       73          3,986     43.1%         1,719
  76+         80            854     46.1%           394
  Total                  99,276                  19,487

  rho = 19,487 / 99,276 = 0.196, rounded to 0.20

Same calculation at other rates (for sensitivity):
  0.5% per year                         -> rho = 0.13
  0.77% per year (Alectra 2021-2024)    -> rho = 0.20
  1.0% per year (Alectra 2027-2031 plan: 5,256 poles over 5 years) -> rho = 0.24

Why 0.20 holds up
  * Not counted: reactive replacements (car hits, storm damage). The DSP only
    reports these as costs and work orders, not pole counts. This would push
    rho up.
  * Pole Renewal also covers concrete poles, so dividing by wood poles alone
    slightly overstates the wood rate, and past decades likely had a lower
    replacement rate (the DSP describes a growing backlog of deteriorated
    poles). These push rho down.
  * The two roughly cancel.

Note: spot-replaced poles get ages from the full histogram, not forced young.
So rho is best described as "the share of poles whose age is independent of
their line section", a correlation setting more than a literal replacement
rate. Suggested sensitivity runs: rho = 0.15, 0.25, plus 0 and 1.0 as the
extremes.


5. CAP AT 60 YEARS FOR THE FRAGILITY LOOKUPS (LIMITATION)
---------------------------------------------------------
The Ma et al. (2020) Fig. 4 and Fig. 7 lookups only cover pole ages 0-60 yr.
About 12% of Alectra's known-age wood poles are older than 60.

  age_true_years  = age assigned from the Alectra distribution (up to 85)
  age_years       = age used by the fragility lookups = min(age_true_years, 60)
  flag_age_capped = True where the age was capped

Treating a 70-year-old pole as 60 UNDERESTIMATES its failure probability.
This should be stated as a limitation. Feeder 1878: 177 of 1,432 poles capped.
